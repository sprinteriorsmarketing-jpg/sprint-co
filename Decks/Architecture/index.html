Villa Chennai

A three-level private residence built around light, stone, and a poolside garden edge. SprintCo led the architecture and execution, the structural envelope, the stacked glass-and-stone facade, and the layered terraces that step back from the street toward a private pool deck at the rear.

Inside, the collaboration continued with 3 Views on the interior design: warm stone cladding and timber joinery on the exterior give way to Athangudi-tile floors, upholstered headboards, and brass detailing indoors, a residence designed to feel handcrafted at every scale, from the facade down to the bedside table.

MG — Emgee Greens

For Emgee Group's 80-acre gated plotted community near Karla, SprintCo designed the architectural centrepiece: a 40,000 sq. ft. clubhouse anchoring a 7-acre golf course — the first between Mumbai and Pune.

The brief called for a resort-scale clubhouse that still reads as an extension of the landscape: layered timber pergolas, deep verandas, and a poolside pavilion designed to frame the site's 22-year-old green cover, restaurant, bar, games room, and sports facilities including padel tennis, pickleball, and cricket.

"100 Shades of Green" Emgee Greens brand positioning.

R Bungalow

Concept in development. R Bungalow is envisioned as a two-level lifestyle dining destination in Bandra: a lively ground-floor café by day, a slower, more immersive dining room above. Rather than demolish the existing two-storey structure, SprintCo proposed a layered independent facade, a lightweight secondary skin anchored to the existing slabs, upgrading the building without major structural intervention.

Three design routes were explored for that facade: The Monolith Mudhouse, a sculptural lime-plaster mass that reads as carved rather than built; The Living Canopy, a vegetation-led facade merging architecture and landscape; and The Porous Pavilion, a breathable terracotta screen that glows like a lantern at night. The gallery below moves from the existing site through all three.

Nest Hotel

Nest Hotel is envisioned as an elegant urban retreat that reinterprets classical architectural character for the modern traveller. Its rhythmic arches, refined proportions and warm stone-toned facade create a timeless identity, while contemporary glazing, subtle greenery and layered lighting bring freshness and sophistication.

The hotel balances heritage-inspired grandeur with warmth, comfort and efficient hospitality, offering guests a welcoming "nest" within the city — from the arched, colonnaded entrance through to guest rooms and bathrooms finished with the same quiet, material-led restraint.

"Contemporary Classical Elegance" — design direction.

The hotel is conceived as a contemporary interpretation of classical architecture, combining rhythmic arches, balanced proportions and refined detailing with clean modern lines. A warm, neutral material palette, subtle greenery and thoughtfully integrated lighting create a timeless urban landmark that feels elegant, welcoming and distinctly memorable.

Rathi Residence

A full-house residential interior for a private family home in Delhi, spanning formal living areas, a suite of bedrooms and a dedicated basement entertainment level. The material language stays consistent throughout: rich walnut and rosewood veneers, carved jali screens, herringbone parquet, and warm cove lighting layered against restrained, tactile backdrops.

The living room is anchored by a laser-cut lattice partition that filters light without closing off the space, while the master and guest bedrooms lean into tufted upholstery, statement chandeliers and picture windows. A daughter's room breaks the palette with a soft floral mural and a built-in window seat, and the basement level turns fully social — a bar, home theatre and lounge finished in weathered metal and reclaimed wood.

Custom joinery carries the detailing further: a book-matched walnut main door sets the tone at the entrance, and a family of bespoke bedroom wardrobe doors, each with a inset photo panel, repeats the same craft logic room to room.

Casa Palma

Casa Palma is a private villa in Kochi built on a simple idea: a Mediterranean shell, softened and opened up for the tropics. Lime-washed stucco walls, terracotta-tiled roofs and arched steel-framed windows give the house a quiet, established character, while deep verandas, a gated palm-lined driveway and generous cross-ventilation keep it firmly rooted in its coastal setting.

Inside, a double-height living room opens through a run of arched French doors onto the front porch, blurring the line between the garden and the interior. A glazed spiral stair rises through the front facade as a lit, sculptural centrepiece, visible from the street after dark. Materials stay warm and restrained throughout, travertine flooring, stone-topped furniture and a muted, art-led palette, so the architecture, not the decor, carries the house.

A second, garden-facing wing shifts the material language toward exposed grey stone and dark timber battens under deep, wood-lined eaves, a quieter counterpoint to the villa's formal front facade. Inside it, a teak-lined reading lounge with built-in shelving and a low daybed opens onto floor-to-ceiling glazing, keeping the same principle of dissolving the boundary between the built and the planted.

Bhatia Villa

Bhatia Villa is a contemporary family residence built from a composition of stacked, shifting boxes rather than a single unified mass. Textured white stucco volumes cantilever over each other at the upper floor, broken up by deep-set black-framed windows, vertical timber-and-metal louvre screens, and a single navy-blue soffit that marks the entrance canopy — the one saturated colour note against an otherwise monochrome facade.

The entry sequence is deliberately layered: a timber-lined carport gives way to a dark pivot door set into a navy-framed recess, screened from the street by a low stucco wall and clipped hedging beneath a canopy of jacaranda trees.

Inside, the architecture opens up. A double-height living room is lit through full-height glazing onto the rear garden, with a floating timber staircase — its treads cantilevered off the wall with no visible stringer — reading as the room's sculptural centrepiece. Warm oak tones, a neutral sectional, leather accent seating and large-format abstract art keep the interior calm and tactile against the exposed concrete-toned flooring.

Reddy Villa

Reddy Villa is a grand Mediterranean-style residence set among mature palms, built around a formal, symmetrical language: lime-plastered stucco walls, a terracotta-tiled hip roof, and tall arched steel-framed windows that repeat rhythmically across both storeys. A covered carport and gated, hedge-lined driveway announce the house well before the front door, in keeping with its established, estate-like presence.

The interior carries the same formality through into scale rather than ornament: a double-height reception room lined with a run of arched French doors that open fully onto the garden, twin wrought-iron chandeliers overhead, and a travertine floor underfoot. A single oversized abstract canvas and a book-matched stone coffee table are the only real punctuation in an otherwise restrained, neutral palette of taupe upholstery and warm stone — letting the arches and the light do the work.
