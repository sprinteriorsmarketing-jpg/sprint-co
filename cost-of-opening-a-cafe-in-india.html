Cost of Opening a Café in India: A Complete Budget Guide

Opening a café costs more than its interiors. This guide breaks the budget into the parts every owner has to plan for, from fit-out and equipment to licences, deposits and the cash you need to survive the first few months, with a worksheet you can fill in for your own site.

Quick answer: the cost of opening a café in India is the sum of interiors and fit-out, coffee and kitchen equipment, licences, rent deposit, branding and technology, opening stock, working capital and a contingency. Interiors alone typically run ₹800–2,500 per sq ft in a metro depending on finish; the rest depends on your menu, site and landlord.

Most first-time café owners plan the interior and the coffee machine, then discover the other half of the budget late. Below, each part of the budget is explained with what drives it, so you can build a realistic number before you sign a lease.

1. Interiors and Fit-Out

This covers civil work, flooring, walls and ceilings, the counter and joinery, furniture, lighting, electrical and plumbing, and signage. It is usually the largest single item. SprintCo’s published benchmarks for interiors on a bare shell in a metro are:

2. Coffee and Kitchen Equipment

The espresso machine and grinders are the heart of a coffee-led café, but the list is longer: brewers, blenders, refrigeration, display chillers, an oven or reheating equipment, dishwashing, water filtration and smallwares. What you need depends entirely on the menu, so fix the menu first and get quotes from two or three suppliers. Our coffee shop kitchen equipment guide and printable equipment checklist help you build the list.

3. Licences and Registrations

FSSAI, GST, shop and establishment, trade licence, fire safety clearance where required, signage and music licences all carry fees, and many owners use a consultant to manage them. See our restaurant licences checklist for the full list.

4. Rent Deposit and Pre-Opening Rent

Landlords usually ask for a security deposit of several months’ rent, and malls may add fit-out deposits and common area charges. You will also pay rent during the build unless you negotiate a rent-free fit-out period. This is one reason a faster build saves money: every month on site is a month of rent with no sales. With FlatPackCo’s off-site fabrication, builds take 50% less time than a traditional build.

5. Branding, Technology and Marketing

Logo and identity, menus, packaging, uniforms, POS and billing software, a website and listings on delivery platforms, plus a launch budget for social media and opening events. Branding and interiors work best when designed together, so the counter, signage and packaging tell one story.

6. Opening Stock and Smallwares

Coffee beans, ingredients, cups, takeaway packaging, crockery and cleaning supplies for the first weeks of trading.

7. Staff Hiring and Training

Salaries during the training period before opening, recruitment costs and barista training.

8. Working Capital

A new café rarely breaks even in its first month. Plan cash for rent, salaries, utilities and stock for the first few months, so slow early weeks do not force cut corners.

9. Contingency

Set aside 10–15% of the total for site surprises, scope changes and price movements. Renovating an existing space or converting a non-F&B unit carries more risk, so use the higher end.

Where Café Budgets Go Wrong

  • Designing before the menu is fixed, then paying to move equipment, power and drainage.
  • Ignoring services: a site without enough electrical load or a drainage point can add a large, unplanned cost.
  • Underestimating the build period: each extra month on site is another month of rent.
  • No working capital: a café that opens with no cash buffer is forced into rushed decisions.
