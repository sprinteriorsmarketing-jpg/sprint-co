01  /  Situation & Challenge

SOCIAL ECity & The Challenge

Electronic City built India’s tech revolution. It never quite figured out where that revolution should hang out.

This is where the first wires were laid and the first code was written, a corridor built for 10-hour shifts and 1 AM cravings. Today it’s a hybrid jungle: young professionals still figuring out how to make Bangalore feel like home, alongside the OGs of tech and biotech who built E-City from the ground up and still carry the energy of boardrooms and breakthroughs.

When SOCIAL approached SprintCo with a third-floor commercial space tucked away in Electronic City, the ask wasn’t a fit-out. It was a vibe shift.

“Turn this office-shaped box into a SOCIAL-shaped experience. And make sure nobody misses it.”

Founder — SOCIAL, Impresario Entertainment & Hospitality

The complication sat in three layers at once: make a hidden, third-floor location feel magnetic; make a rigid, office-shaped space feel rebellious; and make all of it modular, fast and repeatable without losing SOCIAL’s soul in the process.

02  /  Approach

The Strategy & Design Concept

SOCIAL didn’t want “cool.” They wanted chaotic charm, an interior that poked fun at office culture while building something infinitely more alive.

SprintCo built the concept around layered rebellion, not loud rebellion. The space had to say “Anti-Office,” but with a wink. Visual cues were pulled directly from the world it was mocking: corporate clichés, Excel sheets, PPT decks, DOC files became ironic wall art. Office props like file cabinets, office chairs and printers were repurposed as planters. Kannada pop typography was borrowed from film posters and street ads. A real shipping container became the mezzanine centrepiece. Old server rooms inspired the bar’s throwback vibe, and bathroom tiles were intentionally mismatched to reflect deliberate disorder.

The moodboard: corporate clichés, office props and Kannada pop culture, translated into built form.


Nothing was off limits. Everything had a purpose or a punchline. That same instinct carried into branding and signage: the design doesn’t sit quietly in a corner, it roasts the very space it lives in printers turned into planters, a photocopier host desk, spam posters advertising “Learn Kannada in 30 Days,” corporate motivational posters so bad they’re good.

Underneath the satire sat a strict functional brief: the space had to work as hard as it partied.

By Day

  • Flexible workstations and community tables that don’t feel like afterthoughts
  • Pivot tables that rearrange depending on group size or vibe
  • Semi-private rooms for meetings or investor pitches, named after Kannada icons
  • A cantilevered “CEO Room” with a digital stock ticker, the only corner office you actually want to hang out in

By Night

  • The server-room-style bar becomes the glowing heart of the space
  • An infinity mirror DJ console turns on, part nostalgic, part futuristic
  • A tapri-style smoking zone rolls into action, channelling old Bangalore charm
  • Lighting transitions and acoustic shifts transform the floor into a nightlife hub

03  /  Delivery

Execution & Build

SOCIAL ECity wasn’t just a design experiment it was a construction rethink.

Three concrete problems stood between the concept and an opening night, and each was solved with the same build partner that made the timeline possible: FlatpackCo.

Challenge 01

Invisible Entrance

The access point was tucked into a corner behind layers of signage and storefronts, easy to miss, and nowhere near a landmark bar.

Solution

A bold, Flatpack-built facade now juts out from the building, visible from every angle, even the metro line.

Challenge 02

Signboard Chaos

In a commercial strip where everyone is shouting for attention, the space risked getting visually lost among flex boards and hoardings.

Solution

Oversized typography, high-contrast colour and angled panels, executed with Flatpack precision, cut through the noise from every direction.

Challenge 03

Boring Layout

The interior was structured like a standard office: long corridors, boxed-in zones, no sense of discovery and zero SOCIAL energy.

Solution

A cantilevered CEO room, a server-room-style bar and flexible furniture layouts, all built via Flatpack for quick install and future rollouts.

By shifting key components: the bar, mezzanine, furniture and facade: to off-site fabrication, the build became sharper, faster and smarter.

65 Days

Ready to open, against 100+ days for traditional construction, without compromising detail or durability.

30-Hour Bar Build

The 60 ft server-room-style bar counter was pre-engineered and assembled on site in just 30 hours.

Zero Surprises

Every major element was prototyped and finished in the factory, so what was visualised is exactly what got installed.

From the server-room bar to the angular steel façade, every major element was designed to be replicated across future locations letting SOCIAL scale without reinventing the wheel each time.

05  /  Impact

Results & Impact

Designing SOCIAL ECity was never about filling a floor plate. It was about rewriting a story taking a stiff, overlooked third-floor office and turning it into a cultural landmark.

  • A space that felt invisible now draws people in from across the street solved at the façade and signage level, not just inside the door.
  • A layout that once echoed boardroom boredom is now a canvas of contradictions, where irony, chaos and comfort coexist by design.
  • Build timeline cut to 65 days against 100+ days for a traditional build, with a 60 ft bar assembled in 30 hours and zero on-site surprises.
  • A construction method built to repeat — every major element engineered for replication, so SOCIAL can scale this concept without reinventing it each time.
  • Endorsed by Riyaaz Amlani, Managing Director of Impresario Entertainment & Hospitality, as a defining project for the SOCIAL brand.

The project didn’t just land with guests — it caught the attention of the global design community.

The world’s first magazine dedicated exclusively to Food & Beverage spaces featured SOCIAL ECity for its immersive storytelling, cultural layers, and rebellion-in-a-box design approach.

Love That Design — Dubai

This global design and construction platform spotlighted the project for its bold spatial choices, scalable execution, and the Flatpack-driven rethink of traditional F&B builds.
