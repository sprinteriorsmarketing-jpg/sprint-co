The brief

Why airport outlets are different.

Airport F&B is designed for passengers on a clock and built under some of the strictest site rules there are:

  • Speed of service: passengers have minutes, not hours, so ordering, payment and pickup must be quick and obvious
  • Visibility from every direction: outlets are seen from concourses, gates and escalators, as at Cococart
  • Brand recall: bold, simple identity that registers at a glance, like Punjab Grill’s lion and yellow fluted counters
  • Compact footprints: every centimetre of a high-rent unit has to work
  • Operator design guidelines: airport operators set rules on heights, materials, signage and fire safety
  • Security and logistics: worker passes, material screening and airside access shape the build
  • Limited working windows: night shifts and short installation slots, which suit off-site fabrication
  • Comfort before take-off: a familiar, warm feel, as at Tea Tapri, “a taste of home, just before take-off”
How we work

From site to opening day.

  1. Operator brief
    Unit size, services, design guidelines and the approval process set by the airport operator.
  2. Concept and layout
    Service flow, queueing and display designed for passengers in a hurry.
  3. Approval drawings
    Drawings and material specifications prepared for the operator’s review.
  4. Off-site fabrication
    Counters, shells and joinery built in the FlatPackCo factory while approvals and passes are arranged.
  5. Night or short-window install
    Components screened, moved airside and assembled within the permitted hours.
  6. Snag and open
    Final checks, operator sign-off and handover ready for trading.

Budget

What does an airport outlet cost?

Airport outlet budgets depend heavily on the operator’s specifications, security logistics and night working, so they are best priced from the operator brief. As a starting point, most airport outlets are kiosks or compact counters; use the kiosk benchmark below and add allowances for airside logistics:

Finish levelIndicative range
Budget / functional kiosk₹1,040–1,560 per sq ft
Mid-range kiosk₹1,950–3,250 per sq ft
Premium kiosk₹3,640–6,500+ per sq ft

Ranges are lower in tier 2 and tier 3 cities on labour-heavy work. Get a figure for your own area, format and city with the restaurant interior cost calculator.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Which airport projects has SprintCo designed?

Cococart at Mumbai Airport Terminal 2, Streetfoods by Punjab Grill at Bengaluru Airport Terminal 1 and Tea Tapri at Lucknow Airport.

How do you build inside a working airport?

Counters, shells and joinery are fabricated off-site by FlatPackCo, then screened, moved airside and assembled within the permitted working windows, so time on site is short.

Do you work with the airport operator’s design guidelines?

Yes. We design to the operator’s rules on heights, materials, signage and fire safety and prepare drawings for their approval.

How much does an airport F&B outlet cost?

It depends on the operator’s specifications and logistics. As a starting point, use the kiosk benchmark of about ₹1,950–3,250 per sq ft for a mid-range finish, and try the cost calculator.

Can you design a full airport restaurant, not only a kiosk?

Yes. We design restaurants, cafés, bars and QSRs, and apply the same process to larger airport units.

Do you design for other transit locations?

Yes. Kanha Delight Express, a thali QSR inside a railway coach at Jaipur Railway Station, is one example.
