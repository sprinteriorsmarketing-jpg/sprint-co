How to Choose a Restaurant Interior Design Company in India: 12 Things to Check Before You Hire

Quick answer: A restaurant interior design company worth hiring should specialise in F&B rather than general commercial work, show a portfolio relevant to your format, own space planning and kitchen/BOH coordination, handle MEP early rather than as an afterthought, hand you a full technical deliverable list before build begins, and stay accountable through design-to-build handoff. Score every firm you shortlist against the 12 points below before you sign anything.

Why This Decision Matters More Than It Looks

Ask any restaurant owner who has been through a rough fit-out and you will hear some version of the same story: the architect blamed the contractor, the contractor blamed the drawings, the branding agency was doing its own thing on the side, and nobody picked up the phone when the exhaust duct did not fit. Three months late. 25% over budget. And through every one of those months, rent kept running on a dark, empty shell that was not making a rupee.

That is not bad luck, it is what happens when a restaurant is treated like a decoration project instead of an operating system. A restaurant is not a home with a commercial kitchen bolted on the back. It is a live environment where customer flow, kitchen movement, service paths, ventilation, drainage, lighting, acoustics, branding, maintenance and revenue per square foot all have to work together from day one.

The right design partner understands those relationships before the first material is even selected. Use the checklist below to compare firms on that basis, not on whichever render looks best on a screen.

The 12-Point Checklist

1. Confirm real F&B specialisation, not just "commercial" experience

Ask what percentage of the firm's live work is actually restaurants, cafés, bars, QSRs or hospitality. A designer who has done homes, offices or retail may still be talented, but restaurant planning brings its own operational and technical demands. Look for proof the team understands service speed, guest dwell time, front-of-house and back-of-house relationships, exhaust, wash areas, delivery pickup and the wear a high-footfall space takes daily.

2. Judge the portfolio by relevance, not volume

Do not stop at a gallery of beautiful images. Check whether the portfolio includes projects close to your own format. A 400 sq. ft. grab-and-go kiosk needs different thinking from a 3,000 sq. ft. casual dining restaurant or a late-night bar. For every shortlisted project, ask what the brief was, what problem the design actually solved, and whether the team was responsible only for concept or also for technical drawings, MEP, branding and build coordination.

3. Test their space planning before you sign anything

A restaurant layout should support how the business actually runs. Before appointing a firm, walk them through your menu, service model, expected seating, delivery share, peak-hour behaviour and staffing. A strong team can explain why the counter sits where it does, how guests enter and queue, where staff cross the floor, how pickup is separated from dine-in traffic, and what happens on a Saturday night when the place is full.

4. Ask exactly how they plan kitchen and back-of-house

The back of house is invisible to guests, but it decides whether the concept runs smoothly after launch. Your partner should understand how menu, equipment, preparation, cooking, wash-up, storage and utilities connect. If a specialist kitchen consultant is involved, the interior team still needs to coordinate that work with architecture and MEP, rather than treating it as someone else's problem to solve later.

5. Check MEP capability early, not after concept approval

Mechanical, electrical and plumbing coordination is where the most attractive restaurant concepts quietly become expensive to build. Exhaust routes, HVAC, electrical loads, drainage slopes, grease handling, hot water and equipment power points need attention while the layout is still flexible, not after it is locked. Ask whether MEP sits in-house, with an appointed consultant, or is left entirely to the contractor, and then ask who owns the coordination between design and services.

6. Decide if branding should live with the same partner

For a new restaurant, the interior and the brand are usually being built at the same time. If you appoint separate agencies, clarify upfront how logo, colour system, typography, menus, packaging, signage and environmental graphics will stay coordinated with the physical space. A joined-up approach pays off especially for first-time concepts and brands planning to expand, since the identity can be designed to work from a small kiosk through to a larger flagship without being reinvented at every stage.

7. Get the full deliverable list before comparing fees

A concept presentation is not the same as an execution package, and two proposals with similar fees can promise very different amounts of work. Confirm exactly what you will receive before build begins:

  • Proposed layouts and zoning
  • Concept and mood direction
  • 3D visualisations
  • Material and finish information
  • Technical and working drawings
  • MEP layouts or coordination scope
  • BOQ or quantity information, if included
  • Signage and branding interfaces
  • Site coordination and design clarification scope

8. Compare cost coordination, not just the design fee

A lower design fee becomes irrelevant the moment the design keeps changing after contractor pricing comes in. Ask how budget is discussed during design, when quantities get reviewed, and what happens if the approved concept exceeds the intended capex. A commercially aware designer can identify cost-sensitive elements, propose value-engineering options, and tell the difference between design ambition the brand actually needs and detailing that can be simplified without anyone noticing.

9. Understand the design-to-build handoff in writing

Find out exactly what happens after the design is approved. Who appoints contractors. Who checks shop drawings. Who responds to site RFIs. Who reviews samples. Who owns execution quality. If design and build sit with separate teams, ask how information moves between them. If one group handles both, ask how design approvals, commercial controls and execution responsibility are documented, not just promised in conversation.

10. Ask how the timeline is actually built

Do not accept a single opening-date promise without seeing the dependencies behind it. A credible programme shows design approvals, technical coordination, material decisions, procurement, fabrication, site works, installation, testing and snagging laid out against each other. The faster a project moves, the more those early decisions matter. Real speed comes from parallel planning and controlled scope, not from quietly skipping drawings. This is the exact discipline behind SprintCo's own 30 to 45 day delivery window: nothing is skipped, it is simply planned in parallel from day one.

11. Check experience with multi-location rollout

Even if this is your first outlet, ask what happens if the concept works. Can the designer convert your first site into a repeatable kit of parts, drawing standards, finish specifications and adaptable rules for future outlets? For chains and franchise models this becomes critical. The goal is consistency across locations without forcing an identical plan onto a different footprint every time.

12. Clarify what happens after the drawings are issued

Restaurant projects generate questions long after drawings are handed over. Confirm whether the appointed team stays available for clarifications, sample reviews, coordination calls and design-intent checks during execution, and get clear on what is included versus billed as an extra. Clarity at the appointment stage protects the owner and the design team equally.

Red Flags Before You Appoint Anyone

Watch for these during pitch and proposal stage:

  • The conversation stays almost entirely about style and references, with little discussion of operations.
  • The fee looks very low, but the deliverable list is vague or undefined.
  • MEP, kitchen coordination and site constraints get pushed to "after concept approval."
  • Nobody can tell you who answers technical questions during execution.
  • Every portfolio project looks the same, regardless of brand or format.
  • The programme assumes instant approvals with no allowance for procurement or third-party delays.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best restaurant interior design company for a new restaurant setup in India?

There is no single "best" company for every project, the right fit depends on your city, format, site area and budget. Score every shortlisted firm against F&B specialisation, layout planning, MEP capability, technical deliverables, cost coordination and build support, using the checklist above, before you decide.

Should I hire a restaurant specialist or a general interior designer?

For a simple cosmetic refresh, a general commercial designer may be sufficient. For a new restaurant with kitchen, MEP, exhaust, drainage, service-flow and operational planning requirements, F&B-specific experience reduces coordination risk significantly.

What should I ask for in a restaurant interior design proposal?

Ask for a clear scope, stage-wise deliverables, revision policy, timeline assumptions, consultant responsibilities, site-support scope, exclusions, payment milestones and ownership of technical coordination.

How many design firms should I compare before hiring one?

Enough to see different approaches without turning the project into a price auction. Three serious, relevant firms are usually more useful than ten generic quotations.

Should the lowest design fee win the project?

Not automatically. Compare the full scope against the cost of missing services. A proposal that excludes MEP coordination, technical drawings or site support can look cheaper on paper while creating far more cost and delay later.

Can the same company handle both branding and interiors?

Yes, and for new concepts this usually improves consistency across signage, materials, menus, packaging and spatial storytelling. Confirm that both capabilities are genuinely staffed and included in scope, not outsourced without oversight.

How long should a restaurant interior design and build project take in India?

Timelines vary by size and scope, but a well-run project runs design approvals, technical coordination, procurement, site works and snagging in parallel rather than in sequence. Ask any firm you shortlist to show the dependencies behind their opening-date promise, not just the date itself.
