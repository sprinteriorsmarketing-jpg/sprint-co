What Style Should I Choose for My Restaurant?

Selecting a restaurant style depends primarily on your menu and format. It adds to the whole look and experience of the restaurant, and extends well beyond aesthetics into service and functionality.

Selecting a restaurant style depends primarily on your menu and format. It adds to the whole look and experience of the restaurant, and extends beyond aesthetics to incorporate services and functionality.

Classic

Associated with sophistication, elegance, and fine dining. Features velvet chairs, upholstered sofas, and rich curtain drapes. Colour palette: yellows, browns, greens, beige. Materials: velvet, silk, leather. The most expensive to maintain, but consistently sought after by discerning diners.

Contemporary

Reduced ornamentation with clean, straight lines. Suitable for casual restaurants and cafés. Colour palette: wide variety, from bold to soft. Materials: steel, glass, plastic, leather, polished wood.

Minimalist

Driven by the mindful need while also providing a comfortable ambience. Primary elements: solid surfaces, sharp lines, pastel shades. Colours: beige, white, blue, green tints. Materials: wood, cotton, metal. Popular for contemporary casual dining and bakeries.

Industrial

Inspired by old factories and industrial spaces. Best for quick-service restaurants. Materials: steel, aluminium, sturdy metals. Bold and refined, appealing strongly to millennials. Also the most affordable and durable option of the styles here.

Bohemian

Carefree layers of pattern, vintage pieces, natural textures, and beautiful colours. Allows the most self-expression among popular styles. Materials: fabric, wood, bamboo, fur. Ideal for vintage-themed cafés and personalised spaces.

Rustic

Evokes nostalgic, traditional feelings. Emphasises sustainable timber practices and supports local artisans. Colour palette: browns, beige. Materials: wood, bamboo, plants. Suitable for teahouses and tavernas.

Conclusion

Thoughtful interior planning establishes your restaurant's atmosphere from the moment guests walk in. We'd recommend working with professional interior designers to optimise your design strategy against your actual menu and format, rather than picking a style in isolation.
